so the answer to "the essence is supporting all the extension positions"
is: yes for ClientHello extensions.

what "all extension positions" does NOT cover
  The ClientHello is not the whole fingerprint. Three other layers carry
  their own knobs, and the library already handles them:

    cipher list order      (record tls.ciphers)
    curve / key share list (record tls.curves, tls.key_shares)
    signature algorithms   (record tls.sigalgs, tls.sigalg_codes)
    HTTP/2 SETTINGS, WINDOW_UPDATE, header order, Akamai hash
    HTTP/3 SETTINGS, QUIC transport parameters, pseudo/header order

  And two things are not about extension support at all:
    - ALPN set (some uTLS captures were recorded over HTTP/1.1 only)
    - GREASE scope (BoringSSL greases every list; a few captures grease
      only some)

  Those are the only deviations left on Chrome records: 15 alpn notes and
  2 GREASE-scope notes, all on utls_ captures taken over raw HTTP/1.1.

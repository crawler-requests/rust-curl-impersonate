iOS request stacks — what they are and how each is reproduced
date: 2026-11-20

question
  "What request libraries exist on iOS, and how do we emulate all their
  versions?"

the libraries, and why most of them share one fingerprint
  iOS apps do not choose a TLS implementation the way Android apps do: the
  platform hands them CFNetwork. So the library list collapses into two
  actual stacks:

  1. NSURLSession / CFNetwork       the system stack. Alamofire, AFNetworking,
                                    Moya, SwiftNIO's URLs, Combine's
                                    dataTaskPublisher and every hand-rolled
                                    URLSession call end up here.
  2. WebKit / WKWebView / Safari    WebKit's own stack (Network.framework
                                    under it), so a WKWebView-shaped request
                                    looks different from URLSession.
  3. Third-party stacks that ship   Cronet (Chromium's stack),
     their own TLS                   BoringSSL/OpenSSL builds and a few
                                    SDKs. Each needs its own capture.

  The captures prove 1 and 2 are distinct: a Safari 16 ClientHello offers
  20 ciphers, an iOS app's offers 13, and the app set is a strict subset.
  Five app records share one JA4 (t13d1314h2_f57a46bbacb6); the MMS app
  uses an 18-cipher variant (t13d1810) because it pins an older CFNetwork
  shape.

against each record's own capture: 19 of 19 identical.

new this pass: NPN
  utls_ios11 and utls_ios12 were the only iOS records that could not match:
  iOS 11/12 Safari offer NPN (extension 13172), and BoringSSL gates it
  behind a callback nothing was setting. The vendored BoringSSL gained
  `SSL_CTX_set_offer_npn`, records carry `tls.offer_npn`, and both now emit
  13172. utls_360_7_5 benefits too - it was Partial and is Close now.

what cannot be covered, and why
  - Safari 27.x is out (27.1 and 27.2 shipped) and there is no profile for
    it: tls-client v1.18.1 tops out at Safari_IOS_26_0, uTLS at iOS 14.
    A real capture needs an iPhone or a macOS host, neither of which this
    Linux machine can drive. The record set therefore stops at iOS 26.
  - Cronet and apps shipping their own BoringSSL/OpenSSL are not
    represented. Each is a distinct ClientHello and each needs its own
    capture from a device.
  - iOS 15-18 app shapes are not recorded separately from 11-14. The
    captures available show the app stack has only two shapes (14 and 18
    ciphers), both already present, so the gap is in version labels
    rather than in wire bytes - but a per-version capture would need real
    devices.
  - utls_ios11/12 remain HTTP/1.1-only captures (their ALPN is the
    harness's), which is a note on those records, not a missing
    capability.

how to add more iOS versions
  An iOS ClientHello is reachable three ways, in increasing order of
  fidelity: a tls-client or uTLS profile (no device), a WKWebView on a
  macOS host pointed at the echo service, or a real iPhone with a proxy.
  This machine is Linux, so only the first is available here; the records
  above are from that route plus the profile definitions' own captures.

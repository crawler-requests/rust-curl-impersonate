Android records: every one now has a real-device observation, 2026-11-24

Each of the 27 base Android records was checked against a hello a real
Android runtime sent, read off the wire. The per-record table is
record-evidence.txt; this file records how the last three were closed, since
each needed a specific piece of hardware or an internal switch.

android_okhttp7   t12d1208h2_d34a8e72043a_31a7e1922da8
  Needed Android 7 - its signature list carries 0x0301 and 0x0303 (md5 and
  sha1 signatures) that no later TLS stack offers. redroid publishes no
  Android 7 image, but the LAN host has KVM, so an Android 7.0 (API 24)
  emulator was installed there and the probe run: OkHttp 3.12 capped to
  TLS 1.2 sent exactly this hello
  (tools/capture/raw/android_okhttp7_device.json).

android_confirmed2  t12d1210h2_d34a8e72043a_f88f2b2eb673
  The OkHttp hello plus the transport's padding. The 63-character limit is
  per DNS label, not per hostname: a 151-character multi-label name on
  Android 9 pushed the hello past 256 bytes, BoringSSL padded it, and the
  JA4 matched byte for byte
  (tools/capture/raw/android_confirmed2_device.json).

android_zalando     t13d1514h2_8daaf6152771_43ade6aba3df
  The OkHttp hello plus a signed_certificate_timestamp. Reproduced byte for
  byte on an Android 10 emulator: OkHttp 4.12 with bundled Conscrypt 1.4.2,
  certificate transparency enabled through
  SSLParametersImpl.setCTVerificationEnabled (the private switch behind
  SSL_enable_signed_cert_timestamps), session tickets enabled through
  setUseSessionTickets, and a hello long enough that the transport padded
  it (tools/capture/raw/android_zalando_device.json).

  Two steps were needed and each taught something:
    - setCTVerificationEnabled is package-private, so getMethod never found
      it; getDeclaredMethod plus setAccessible does.
    - Conscrypt 2.9 sends a 1216-byte X25519MLKEM768 key share, which
      inflates the hello past the 511-byte padding window. 1.4.2 predates
      that group and its hello lands in the window, so the padding appears.

What is still outside the library's records
  The recordings of specific third-party apps remain what they are: shapes
  captured once by tls.peet.ws. Those with a user agent of okhttp/4.12.0
  turned out to be ordinary OkHttp hellos and are confirmed as such
  (android_confirmed, android_confirmed2, android_zalando). android_nike
  and android_mesh are likewise OkHttp/WebView shapes already verified
  through the records they equal.

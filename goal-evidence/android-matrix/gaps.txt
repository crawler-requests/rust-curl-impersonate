Android records still without a real-device observation, 2026-11-24

The audit below compares each Android record's c hash with every c hash a
real device produced in this work: the 33-build cloud-phone grid, the phone
captures, the TLS 1.2 capping runs and the WebView service reports. 25 of
27 base records are backed by a real device - 23 matched a captured hello
byte for byte, and android_webview125 and android_webview139 were captured
(one of them server-classified) on real hardware. Two have no backing:

android_okhttp7   t12d1208h2_d34a8e72043a_31a7e1922da8
  OkHttp on Android 7: TLS 1.2, 8 extensions, no session_ticket, no
  status_request, and signature algorithms without the rsa_pss family.
  No Android 7 runtime can be booted here (no redroid image for it; the
  OnePlus 9R is Android 13). The record stays a peet capture with
  fidelity "close" because BoringSSL cannot send two of its signature
  algorithms either.

android_okhttp8   t12d1209h2_d34a8e72043a_e08eabe7240f   NOW CONFIRMED
  Reproduced byte for byte: OkHttp 3.12 capped to TLS 1.2 on an Android 8.1
  redroid container sent exactly this hello, all three segments equal
  (tools/capture/raw/android_okhttp8_device.json). The Android 8.1 platform
  stack alone also matches its extension list and signature algorithms.

an Android-version difference worth naming
  OkHttp 3.12 on Android 13 sends no ALPN extension (t13d151100); the same
  build on Android 8.1 sends one, with h2 (t12d1209h2). So the ALPN gap is
  the platform's, not the library's, and android_okhttp3_12's t13d151100 is
  a true Android 13 observation. The Android 8.1 capture is the one that
  confirms android_okhttp8.
